# BAC STORY — Algerian Market Strategy

> Deep market analysis of Algerian consumer psychology, applied to the BAC STORY landing page and brand voice.

---

## 1. The seven commercial truths about the Algerian Bac student-buyer

**Trust is geographic and tribal, not institutional.** The Algerian buyer trusts in this exact order: family > tribe (3aroush, classmates, neighborhood) > wilaya > religion > Algerian brands > foreign brands. *Strangers selling things online start at zero credibility.* Marketing copy that doesn't anchor in named, located, recognizable people — Islem himself, students from named lycées, results from named wilayas — gets ignored. Generic "5,000 students love us" claims read as foreign-brand boilerplate and lose trust the moment they're seen.

**"Free" is suspicious until proven.** *كي يجي free 3andek 7aja makitkounch sahla* — when something is free there's usually a catch. The local consumer instinctively assumes a free product hides a paid trap. The way to neutralize this is to be loud about *why* the library is free: it's free because we make our money on the AI subscription, and explicit about what stays free forever. "مجاني للأبد" must be paired with "بدون كارت بنكية" and "بدون مفاجآت" or it sounds like a hook.

**One-time payments beat subscriptions, especially for parent-funded purchases.** Algerian parents will spend 5,000–15,000 DZD on a *stage intensif* before the Bac without flinching, but resist 999 DZD/month subscriptions because a recurring charge feels like a leak. The 4,500 DZD Bac-season SKU is mentally categorized as a *stage*, not a *subscription*. **This must be the lead price on the pricing page**, with the monthly tier presented as the alternative for non-Bac-year users.

**Hgra is a real phenomenon and your brand must actively counter it.** Algerian users have been treated as a second-tier market by foreign tech for years (no Apple Pay, products launched 18 months late, prices sometimes shown in EUR). They notice the slightest hint of being talked down to. The brand voice must address them as smart adults who already know what they're doing, just need the right tools. Never use phrases like "learn how to study properly" or "discover the right method" — those imply they've been doing it wrong. Better: "نفس الطريقة لي عليها قراو الناجحين، مرتبة لك."

**Sma3 w shouf — they trust what they can see, not what they read.** Written promises are heavily discounted. Photos, names, screenshots, video, *specific* numbers move the needle dramatically. A testimonial with first name + lycée name + wilaya + a real photo converts ten times harder than a generic five-star review. Plan to collect those *with photos* — even one strong testimonial with a face beats five anonymous ones.

**The successful local brands speak as peers, not authorities.** Cevital, Condor, Ramy, Numidia, Ifri — the brands that actually own Algerian shelf space all talk to consumers as equals. They don't lecture. They don't condescend. They use Darija when it lands and Arabic when it elevates. They're proud of being Algerian without being defensive. BAC STORY's voice should sit in this exact register: confident peer, not lecturing professor.

**Religion is the third rail — touch it lightly, never as a sales hook.** *إن شاء الله* and *ربي يوفقك* belong in motivational moments (after a student does well, in onboarding emails, in encouraging copy). They do *not* belong in headlines, pricing pages, or sales-driven CTAs. Heavy religious framing in commercial contexts feels manipulative and triggers immediate distrust. The current AI persona handles this perfectly — extend the same restraint to all marketing.

## 2. What "premium" looks like in the Algerian market specifically

Premium does not mean expensive imagery or luxury flourishes. In the Algerian commercial context, premium is signaled by four things: confident typography (large, calm, not shouty), generous whitespace (Algerian sites are usually dense; restraint signals self-assurance), specific evidence (named people, real numbers, dated screenshots), and visible respect for local context (CIB/EDAHABIA/BaridiMob shown alongside or before international cards, prices in DZD with no euro conversion, Arabic-first with French as a courtesy not the default).

What signals *cheap* in this market: cartoon illustrations of generic students, animated mascots, neon-bright color combinations, stock photography of foreign-looking professionals smiling at laptops, multiple competing CTAs on one screen, trust badges that look bought ("Best in 2025!"), and any copy translated literally from English templates.

The visual upgrade for BAC STORY is therefore *editorial*, not *flashy*. Think *Le Quotidien d'Oran* meets *Apple Education*: large confident headlines, real photos of Algerian students at real desks with real cahiers, deep navy hero on which the brand blue and orange pop without competing, gold reserved entirely for achievement moments. No gradients beyond the existing subtle hero gradient. No drop shadows except functional focus rings. No animated "tada!" effects on scroll.

## 3. The new content structure (8 sections, ordered for psychological flow)

The 5-section structure I proposed earlier is structurally sound but skips the conversion-critical *recognition* moment. The Algerian buyer needs to feel seen *before* they listen to the product pitch. The new ordering applies the AIDA framework but tuned for local psychology:

**Section 1 — Hero.** A two-line proverb-like truth + the bridge to your product. Not a feature list. Real numbers strip below (X students, X wilayas, X messages answered). One animated chat card showing the AI mid-reply. One CTA in orange.

**Section 2 — The pain mirror.** A short paragraph in Darija that names back what the student is going through. *"تعرف هاد الإحساس كي تقعد ليل تحفظ، تروح للفرض و راسك يفرغ؟ تحوس على ملخصات مظبوطة، تلقى 50 قناة تيليجرام و كل واحدة تقول حاجة."* This is the moment of recognition. Do not skip it.

**Section 3 — The product (modes + library combined).** Show the four AI modes as one row, then a horizontal scroll of library cards underneath. Both in one section because they're one product. Tag every library item with "مجاني" to hammer home the model.

**Section 4 — Real students.** Three to six testimonials with photos, names, lycées, wilayas. This is your single strongest credibility asset. Design space for them now.

**Section 5 — Islem's note.** A short founder section: photo of Islem, three sentences in his voice, no marketing language. *"كنت مكانك، ما لقيتش حد يفهمني. هكا بنيت BAC STORY."* This is parasocial trust extension from the Telegram channel to the website.

**Section 6 — Pricing teaser.** Three cards: Free, Plus 999 DZD/شهر, Premium 1990 DZD/شهر — but the **Bac-season 4500 DZD** package featured prominently as the recommended SKU. Include the local payment methods row (CIB / EDAHABIA / BaridiMob logos) below the cards as a respect signal.

**Section 7 — FAQ (Algerian-specific objections).** Five questions answered concretely: هل يحتاج كارت بنكية؟ كيفاش ندفع بدينار؟ هل تشتغل بدون نت؟ هل دارجة فعلاً ولا ترجمة؟ شنو يصير بمعطياتي؟ Each answer is two sentences max, written by Islem in his voice.

**Section 8 — Final CTA.** White background, large headline, single orange button, three concrete objection killers underneath ("free forever / no card needed / sign up in 30 seconds").

## 4. The voice — concrete copy guidelines

Write headlines in Arabic that feel like proverbs, not slogans. *"الباك ما تنجاز بالحفظ. تنجاز بالطريقة."* lands harder than *"احصل على أعلى الدرجات في البكالوريا."* The first is something a wise older brother would say. The second is something a foreign EdTech translated into Arabic.

Keep buttons concrete and action-first. *"إنشاء حساب — 30 ثانية"* outperforms *"ابدأ الآن."* The duration sets expectations and lowers anxiety.

Numbers should always be specific and recent. *"6,247 طالب من 41 ولاية"* outperforms *"آلاف الطلبة."* If you don't have a number yet, don't fabricate one — leave the slot empty until launch.

When you have to use English/French words because no clean Darija equivalent exists (AI, Telegram, Bac), use them naturally without italics or apology. Algerians code-switch constantly; pretending otherwise looks performative.

Never use *"اكتشف"* or *"تعرف على"* in CTAs — these are direct translations from English/French and instantly read as foreign-brand copy. Use action verbs that match how a Telegram caption would read: *"شوف"*, *"جرب"*, *"ابدأ"*, *"احسبها"*.

Avoid emoji clusters in headlines. One emoji at a section header maximum. Algerian premium content (newspapers, books, real journalism) doesn't use emoji; emoji-heavy copy reads as social-media-content not platform-content.

## 5. Trust signals to include before launch

A real footer with: Algerian company info (RC if applicable, even informal), a real phone or Telegram contact, a privacy policy that mentions data is stored on Supabase (yes, name the vendor — transparency converts), and a "made by Algerian developers" line.

Local payment method logos visible *above* international card logos on the pricing page. CIB and EDAHABIA logos on the landing footer as a passing trust signal even before they reach pricing.

A subtle "as seen on" strip if you have any media coverage — even a Facebook post from a known teacher counts. If you don't yet, a "seen by 6,000+ Telegram subscribers" strip works as a substitute.

A clear privacy line on every form: *"معطياتك ما تنشرش، و ما تباعش لحد."* Algerian users have been conditioned by years of leaked databases and shady SMS marketing to fear giving their phone number; saying explicitly that you won't sell or publish data lifts conversion meaningfully.

## 7. The premium upgrade summary

The previous wireframe was correct in structure but read as *startup landing page* rather than *Algerian premium platform*. The upgrades that move it to premium are: a deep navy hero that telegraphs prestige, editorial typography with proper scale jumps (52/22/16, not 22/16/13), more vertical whitespace per section (spacious, not dense), a founder section that bridges Islem's existing trust to the website, pricing visible on the landing (Algerian buyers want to see prices early — hidden pricing reads as foreign-startup behavior), Algerian-specific FAQ as a trust converter, and local payment methods shown as a respect signal.

Everything else from the previous wireframe — the four-modes grid, the AI demo card, the testimonials with named lycées, the orange CTA — those were already correct. We keep them, we just dress them better.
